Just-in-time infrastructure

Infrastructure that appears when an app deploys, and goes when it does.

A proof of concept. One annotation on a Deployment, and the database it needs provisions itself. Nothing is cleaned up on its own: deleting the app starts a grace period, and deleting the namespace skips it.

Kubernetes · kopf · OpenTofu · k3d · Docker

The problem

Apps and their infrastructure have different lifecycles.

Ask A ticket, a Terraform PR, a week.
Forget The environment is torn down. The database is not.
Pay Nobody deletes anything, in case somebody needs it.

The app knows what it needs. Nothing lets it say so.

The ask

Three lines of annotation.

kind: Deployment
metadata:
  name: vote
  annotations:
    jit.infra/redis:    '{"maxmemory":"128mb"}'
    jit.infra/postgres: '{"db":"voting"}'
    jit.infra/pgadmin:  '{}'

No Terraform, no CRD to learn, no service catalogue. The tenant writes what the app needs, in the file that already says what the app is.

What happens

Real infrastructure, outside the cluster, reachable from inside it.

The shape

Two planes, split on purpose.

The controller owns the lifecycle

Watches annotations, decides what should exist and for how long. It holds no cloud credentials and never talks to Docker.

The runner owns the provisioning

Takes an authenticated call, runs versioned Terraform modules, reports back. It knows nothing about claims or namespaces.

The thing with the credentials makes no decisions. The thing making decisions holds no credentials.

A detail worth stealing

There is no “wait for infrastructure” step.

The pod needs a Secret that does not exist yet, so Kubernetes refuses to start it. When the database is ready and the Secret is written, the pod starts on its own.

The absence is the gate. Nothing polls, nothing sleeps, nothing has a timeout to tune.

The centrepiece

Cleanup has two speeds.

Delete the app

A clock starts

The container keeps running. Redeploy inside the window and the same database comes back, with its data. A rollout costs nothing.

Delete the namespace

Gone immediately

The clock is ignored. Deleting a whole environment is a deliberate act in a way that deleting a Deployment is not.

Accidents get a grace period. Decisions do not.

Why it is safe

It counts references by recounting them.

Two apps asking for the same database get one database, and it only starts its clock when the last one goes.

There is no counter to get out of step. Every pass asks the cluster who is using what, and believes the answer.

Why it stays safe

Restart the controller mid-delete and nothing leaks.

It misses the event, then works out the truth on the next pass and starts the clock late, the safe direction to be wrong in.

A destroy that fails holds its finalizer rather than forgetting the container existed. The system would rather stall visibly than lose track.

Honestly

What it deliberately does not do.

No IAM The runner has unrestricted access to its daemon.
No approvals Anything annotated is provisioned at once.
No locking Two applies could race over one workspace.
No backups The retention window is the only safety net.

It is a proof of a lifecycle, not a platform. The interesting part is the state machine, and that part is finished.
